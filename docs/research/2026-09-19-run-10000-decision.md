# Research Decision — Run 10,000 Statistical Communication

**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Category:** Simulation / data explanation

## Problem
Run 10,000 correctly distinguishes a single 10,000-run sample from the model expectation, but the final mobile state gives too much simultaneous weight to secondary statistics and small explanatory text.

A person must immediately understand:
1. what happened in this sample
2. what the calibrated model expects over repeated play

## Relevant experiences inspected
Fidelity retirement analysis; Vanguard Monte Carlo/probability-of-success communication; Schwab retirement income calculator; Wealthfront Path; Empower Retirement Planner; WHOOP Healthspan; Apple Fitness trends; Strava Progress; Monzo Trends; Revolut analytics.

## Patterns that repeat
1. Lead with the decision-relevant result.
2. Separate observed/current from projected/expected.
3. Simulation/projection labels need plain-language limitations.
4. Complex probability scores are easily misunderstood.
5. Visuals help some comparisons, but no single chart automatically fixes comprehension.

Vanguard’s recent research shows “probability of success” can feel intuitive while still being misunderstood. Risk-communication research likewise finds format matters and graphics can help some comparison tasks without eliminating interpretation errors.

## Anti-patterns
Do not imply one sample is the expectation, imply the expectation is a guarantee, carry critical meaning only in tiny text, give every stat equal weight, or hide sample/model distinction behind methodology.

## Spin Out decision
Final state becomes a two-part hero:

**THIS 10,000-RUN SAMPLE**  
[net result]

**WHAT THE MODEL EXPECTS OVER TIME**  
[expected result]

Then one plain sentence explaining why they can differ. Wins/losses/near misses/longest streak/staked/returned/strategy note become a clearly secondary breakdown.

Keep 1 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 progression because it teaches variance through accumulation.

### One thing gets to be loud
**LOUD:** sample versus long-run expectation.  
**SUPPORTING:** one sentence on variance/model limits.  
**QUIET:** supporting counts/methodology.

## Success criteria
- users can identify sample vs expectation without reading fine print
- critical explanation not carried by sub-11px text
- two numbers do not visually merge into one score
- expectation explicitly framed as model output, not guarantee
- secondary stats remain available
- no 320px clipping
- reduced motion lands on same understandable final state
- synthetic testing separately measures sample/expectation distinction and uncertainty comprehension
