# Research Decision — High-Urge / Low-Patience First Run

**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Category:** High urgency / low attention onboarding

## Problem
The five mandatory questions give the first Reality Run useful anchors, but high-urge/low-patience users remain the segment most likely to abandon before gameplay.

The goal is to make the five feel faster without deleting context that materially improves interventions.

## Current behavior
Known-game route:
1. amount at risk
2. hoped-for outcome
3. real-life use of money
4. current urge
5. stopping intention

Each selection auto-advances after short motion. Then the real game is visible and Start Reality Run begins play.

## Relevant patterns inspected
Netflix optional starter preferences; Spotify steering plus passive behavior; Strava one focus + optional detail; Monzo explicit unavoidable-setup time expectation; Uber prioritizing likely choices; Airbnb small necessary search inputs then deeper filters; Duolingo one guided next step; Notion mobile shortcuts; YouTube/Pinterest behavioral personalization; WHOOP proactive check-ins at relevant moments.

## Research
Recent consumer/HCI studies show motion can alter perceived waiting time, with moderate-speed motion often outperforming static/overly fast states. Questionnaire research links burden to length but also to content relevance. JITAI work emphasizes receptivity and avoiding excess prompting when attention is low.

## Spin Out decision
Keep the five mandatory interactions for now.

Optimize perceived effort:
- one idea per screen
- one-tap choices wherever possible
- no redundant explanatory copy
- immediate selection feedback
- fast but perceptible transition motion
- known game removes redundant game question
- support copy visually subordinate
- no unnecessary confirmation buttons
- no progress counter

Do not claim a specific completion time unless measured.

## Measurement
Track interaction count and browser-estimated seconds to game; high-urge abandonment; guessed/wrong answers; perceived effort; first-run intervention quality.

## Success criteria
- exactly five mandatory known-game interactions
- no extra confirmation step
- no task-count pressure
- auto-advance remains
- no required free text
- money-support copy remains subordinate
- 320px-safe
- high-urge synthetic abandonment does not worsen
- do not reduce questions until one proves lower intervention value than friction cost
