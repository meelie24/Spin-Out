# SPIN OUT: MAKE THE PURPOSE OBVIOUS WITHOUT CHANGING THE DESIGN

Keep the current visual direction.

Do not redesign the homepage.
Do not tone down the casino artwork.
Do not remove the Chinese casino influence.
Do not flatten the games.
Do not replace the brown luxury look.
Do not add a new anti-gambling slogan to the logo or background.

The casino visuals are staying.

What needs to change is how Spin Out speaks to the user and reacts to what they're doing.

The goal is simple:

**Within the first few minutes, it should be obvious that the games are being used to help the person understand their gambling behavior and get better at stopping.**

It should never feel like a normal casino website that happens to show warning messages.

The intervention system needs to feel like the actual product.

---

# 1. TURN THE RED PROMPTS INTO A REAL SYSTEM

The red prompts should have a name inside the product:

**Reality Ping**

Use that name consistently.

Don't call them alerts.
Don't call them warnings.
Don't call them responsible-gambling messages.

A Reality Ping should feel like Spin Out noticing something and calling it out.

Example:

Small label:

`REALITY PING`

Main message:

`You pressed again before the last result even settled.`

The label can stay small. The actual message gets the attention.

Keep the same premium red treatment already planned.

Don't redesign them into generic notification cards.

---

# 2. STOP SHOWING RANDOM PROMPTS

Reality Pings should usually have a reason for appearing.

Tie them to what the user just did.

Track useful events such as:

- rapid replay
- repeated losses
- repeated wins
- near misses
- increasing the simulated amount
- staying longer than planned
- continuing after saying they wanted to stop
- repeatedly dismissing Reality Pings
- returning immediately after leaving
- chasing after a loss
- playing faster as the session continues

Each event can trigger a different type of message.

### Example: rapid replay

User presses SPIN again almost immediately.

Show:

`That was fast. Did you even decide to spin again?`

### Example: loss chasing

User loses several rounds and keeps going.

Show:

`Are you trying to get it back now?`

### Example: near miss

The result looks close to a win.

Show:

`That looked close. It wasn't.`

### Example: increasing the amount

User increases the simulated amount after losing.

Show:

`You lost, then raised it. What were you expecting the bigger spin to fix?`

### Example: long session

User has stayed much longer than they normally do.

Show:

`You've been here 11 minutes. Were you planning to stay this long?`

The prompt should make sense because of what just happened.

Don't cycle through a random quote library.

---

# 3. USE MORE QUESTIONS

A lot of the Reality Pings should make the user answer something in their own head.

Keep them short.

Avoid lecture language.

Avoid therapy language.

Avoid corporate responsible-gambling language.

### Good

`Would you still press that if the $50 was sitting beside you?`

`What are you trying to win back right now?`

`Were you actually done, or did the loss change your mind?`

`If this were your real balance, would you keep going?`

`Would you tell somebody you spent that?`

### Bad

`Remember that gambling can have harmful financial consequences.`

### Bad

`Please take a moment to reflect on your gambling behavior.`

Nobody talks like that.

---

# 4. CONNECT SIMULATED MONEY TO REAL LIFE

This needs to become one of Spin Out's strongest features.

Where the user has given us enough information, translate simulated gambling behavior into things that matter in their actual life.

Use information collected during onboarding or Reality Run.

Examples could include:

- car payment
- rent
- phone bill
- groceries
- savings goal
- debt payment
- money left until payday

### Example

User told Spin Out:

Car payment: `$430`
Due: `Tuesday`

They lose `$100` in the simulation.

Reality Ping:

`That's $100 of the $430 car payment you said is due Tuesday.`

Then:

`How were you getting that back?`

### Another example

User has `$300` available until payday.

After enough simulated losses:

`You'd have $86 left.`

Don't turn every result into a financial lesson.

Use these moments when the number actually lands.

---

# 5. USE THE USER'S OWN REASON FOR QUITTING

During onboarding, ask one short question:

**Why are you trying to stop?**

Let them answer in their own words.

Save the answer.

Don't constantly repeat it.

Use it occasionally when their behavior matches the reason they gave.

### Example

User wrote:

`I'm tired of hiding where my money went.`

Later, during a longer session:

`You said you're tired of hiding where the money went.`

Then:

`Would this be another one?`

### Example

User wrote:

`I want to save for a house.`

After a simulated loss:

`You said you're trying to save for the house.`

Keep this rare enough that it still lands.

Do not make every prompt feel like the app is quoting the user back at them.

---

# 6. EXPLAIN WHY EACH GAME EXISTS

Keep the beautiful game cards.

Keep the casino artwork.

Add one short line that tells the user why that simulation is there.

This copy should sit naturally under the game title or inside the card information.

### Slots

`See what keeps you chasing.`

### A game built around near misses

`Catch what "almost" does to you.`

### A fast repeated-spin game

`See how quickly one more becomes another.`

### A game built around stopping

`Practice leaving while you still want another round.`

Keep these short.

The homepage should make it obvious that these games have a purpose beyond entertainment.

---

# 7. LET THE GAMES TEACH THE MECHANICS WHILE THEY HAPPEN

Reality Pings shouldn't only talk about consequences.

Some should expose the gambling mechanic the user just reacted to.

### Near miss

`Your brain reads "almost" differently. The result is still a loss.`

### Losing streak

`Three losses don't make the next spin more likely to win.`

### Win after losses

`This is the kind of win that can make the losses feel worth chasing.`

### Faster play

`You're making the decisions faster now.`

### Increasing the amount

`The amount went up after the loss.`

Keep these plain.

Don't write them like textbook definitions.

Where a short follow-up explanation is useful, let the user tap something like:

`Why did I do that?`

Then explain it in more detail outside the main Reality Ping.

---

# 8. MAKE THE INTERVENTIONS CHANGE WITH THE SESSION

The messages shouldn't have the same tone five seconds into the game and twelve minutes into it.

Use a progression.

Early session:

`You've spun three times.`

A little later:

`You said you'd stop after five.`

After they pass their own limit:

`You're past the point you chose.`

If they continue:

`What are you waiting to happen before you leave?`

If the behavior keeps going:

`Leave now.`

The system should respond to the actual session.

Don't make it dramatic immediately.

Let the pressure build because of what the person is doing.

---

# 9. LET USERS SET A LIMIT BEFORE PLAYING

Before a game begins, occasionally ask something small such as:

`How many rounds are you giving this?`

or:

`How long are you staying?`

Keep it quick.

No long form.

No setup wizard.

Example:

`5 spins`

Then track it.

When they reach five:

`That's the five you chose.`

If they continue:

`You decided on five. This is six.`

Then:

`What changed?`

That turns the user's own decision into part of the intervention.

---

# 10. MAKE LEAVING A SUCCESSFUL ACTION

Do not treat exiting a game like closing a browser tab.

Leaving is one of the behaviors Spin Out is trying to strengthen.

When the user leaves, acknowledge it.

Keep it understated.

### Example

`You left.`

Then show something useful:

`4m 18s`

Previous comparable session:

`8m 42s`

### Another example

`You stopped after 6 rounds.`

Last time:

`14`

No fireworks.

No fake congratulations.

The numbers can speak for themselves.

---

# 11. TRACK WHETHER THEY'RE LEAVING SOONER

Spin Out should care about how long the user stays in the simulations.

Track:

- session length
- rounds played
- time between rounds
- how far they went past a chosen limit
- whether they left after a Reality Ping
- whether session length is getting shorter over time

Show useful progress when a pattern exists.

### Example

`Your last 3 sessions`

`11:14`
`7:02`
`4:36`

Supporting copy:

`You're leaving sooner.`

Don't turn this into an XP system.

Don't add leaderboards.

Don't reward people for playing more.

The improvement should point toward needing the simulation less.

---

# 12. MAKE THE EXIT CONTROL MATTER

Every game should have an easy, obvious way to leave.

It shouldn't feel like the product is hiding the exit because it wants another round.

The control still has to fit the premium design.

Examples:

`I'm done`

`Leave game`

`I'm out`

Choose language that fits the existing Spin Out voice.

Do not use:

`Cash Out`

That sounds like a real gambling product.

When the user chooses to leave, don't put another persuasive modal in their way.

Let them leave.

---

# 13. REALITY PINGS SHOULD SOMETIMES CHANGE THE GAME FLOW

The strongest interventions shouldn't always be passive text.

Some moments should require a tiny break in the loop.

### Example

After unusually rapid repeated spins:

Game pauses briefly.

Reality Ping:

`You haven't stopped long enough to think between the last four.`

Button:

`Keep going`

Secondary option:

`I'm done`

Do not do this constantly.

Most Reality Pings can remain dismissible.

Use stronger interruption only when the session behavior gives a reason.

---

# 14. DON'T LET USERS AUTO-DISMISS EVERYTHING WITHOUT THE SYSTEM NOTICING

Track repeated dismissals.

Do not punish the user.

Do not block them because they closed a message.

Change the message when it becomes relevant.

### Example

After several immediate dismissals:

`You've closed the last four without reading them.`

Then stop.

Don't add a paragraph underneath it.

The observation itself is enough.

---

# 15. CHANGE THE MESSAGE AFTER A SIMULATED WIN

A normal casino treats a win as the emotional peak.

Spin Out should use that exact moment.

The win can still look and sound good.

Let the result land.

Then interrupt the interpretation.

### Example

Win animation finishes.

Brief pause.

Reality Ping:

`This is the part that makes the losses feel worth it.`

or:

`Would this win make you stay longer?`

or:

`How much would you give back trying to hit that again?`

Do not remove the polished win presentation.

The contrast is what makes the moment useful.

---

# 16. DO THE SAME THING WITH NEAR MISSES

Near misses need their own intervention logic.

If the game creates a visually close result:

Reality Ping can acknowledge exactly what happened.

### Example

`It looked one symbol away.`

Then:

`The result wasn't closer to winning. It was still a loss.`

Keep it short.

Don't explain probability theory inside the prompt.

A user who wants more can open the explanation.

---

# 17. USE PERSONAL NUMBERS WHEN WE HAVE THEM

Avoid vague copy when the system knows the real number.

### Weak

`You've spent a lot.`

### Better

`You've put 180 credits through this session.`

### Stronger when onboarding supports it

`That's the same $180 you said you were trying to keep for Friday.`

Specificity makes the intervention feel real.

---

# 18. STOP USING GENERIC RESPONSIBLE-GAMBLING LANGUAGE

Audit all user-facing copy.

Remove phrases that sound like:

- `Play responsibly`
- `Know your limits`
- `Take a break`
- `Gambling can be harmful`
- `Remember to stay in control`
- `Please gamble responsibly`

Spin Out isn't a casino covering itself with a disclaimer.

The language should respond to what the person is actually doing.

### Instead of

`Remember to know your limits.`

Use:

`You chose five. This is eight.`

That is much harder to ignore.

---

# 19. KEEP PROMPTS SHORT

Most Reality Pings should fit comfortably on a phone without becoming a reading exercise.

Aim for one thought.

Sometimes two short lines.

### Good

`You lost, then doubled it.`

`What were you trying to fix?`

### Too much

A full paragraph explaining chasing behavior, probability, financial consequences and impulse control.

Longer education belongs behind a tap.

---

# 20. DON'T OVERUSE THEM

More prompts does not automatically make the product more effective.

Build frequency rules.

Avoid showing a Reality Ping every single round.

Use:

- event importance
- session history
- recent prompt history
- repeated behavior
- cooldowns

to decide whether one should appear.

### Example

If the user receives a loss-chasing prompt, don't show another generic loss prompt on the very next round unless something meaningfully changes.

The user should still notice these.

Don't train them to close red boxes automatically.

---

# 21. AVOID REPEATING THE SAME COPY

Create message families around different behaviors.

Examples:

### Rapid play family

`That was quick.`

`Did you actually decide to go again?`

`You're pressing faster now.`

### Chasing family

`Are you trying to get it back?`

`The loss changed what you were willing to risk.`

`What number are you trying to get back to?`

### Time family

`You've been here 9 minutes.`

`Were you planning to still be playing?`

`You're past the time you chose.`

Use variations intelligently.

Don't randomly shuffle them.

Choose based on context.

---

# 22. USE THE HOMEPAGE PROMPTS DIFFERENTLY FROM IN-GAME REALITY PINGS

The existing scattered red homepage prompts stay.

They should act like small pieces of thought before the person even enters a game.

Examples:

`What usually happens after "one more"?`

`How much are you actually trying to get back?`

`What made you open this today?`

`When do you usually realize you've gone too far?`

`What's the number you keep trying to get back to?`

These should still appear in different positions around the homepage and remain individually dismissible.

They are allowed to be quieter than an in-game Reality Ping.

Do not place them over active gameplay.

---

# 23. CONNECT HOMEPAGE QUESTIONS TO LATER GAMEPLAY WHERE POSSIBLE

If the user actually answers something, use it later.

### Example

Homepage asks:

`What number are you trying to get back to?`

User answers:

`$600`

Later in the simulation:

`Is this about getting back to $600 again?`

Don't collect answers simply because we can.

Only ask for information that can make the intervention better.

---

# 24. BUILD A SIMPLE SESSION SUMMARY AFTER THE USER LEAVES

When someone leaves a game, show a compact summary.

Keep it visually consistent with the current premium UI.

Example:

`4m 18s`

`7 rounds`

`2 Reality Pings`

`You left after the second one.`

Then one useful observation:

`Last time you stayed 8m 42s.`

That's enough.

Don't turn this into a giant analytics dashboard after every game.

---

# 25. MEASURE WHETHER THE INTERVENTION IS WORKING

Track behavior that helps us understand whether people are disengaging sooner.

At minimum:

- session duration
- rounds per session
- time between rounds
- Reality Ping shown
- Reality Ping type
- Reality Ping dismissed
- game exited after Reality Ping
- chosen limit
- chosen limit exceeded
- amount of time past limit
- repeat session timing
- session length trend

Do not use these metrics to optimize for more spins or longer sessions.

The important outcomes are things like:

- shorter sessions
- fewer rounds
- more exits after recognizing a behavior
- fewer sessions exceeding the user's chosen limit

### Increased amount
